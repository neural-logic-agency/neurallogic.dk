ENDEDA

For property and asset management.

Everything about one property, one tenancy or one dispute, assembled across every archive you keep, quoted from the original, and clear about what was never filed.

The problem in this trade

A property carries decades of paper: the purchase, the leases and their amendments, the handover protocols, the defect lists, the correspondence with tenants and contractors, the insurance case nobody closed, the invoices that belong to a repair somebody remembers differently. It sits in a data room, a mail archive and a shared drive, and nobody has time to read all three.

So the question “what exactly did we agree about this roof, and when” costs an afternoon, and is answered from memory more often than anyone admits.

What the colleague does with it

  • The complete file for one object. Everything about a property, a unit or a tenancy, in date order, across every archive in scope.
  • The clause that decides it, quoted. Which lease version governs, what it says about the obligation in question, word for word, with a link to the page it came from.
  • What is missing, named. The handover protocol that was never signed, the amendment referred to but absent, the certificate that expired. From the register, not from a guess.
  • The history of a dispute. Every message, protocol and invoice that touches a case, assembled before a lawyer is paid to assemble it.

What it does not do

It makes no statements about people: no scores on tenants, no predictions about who will pay. Files on buildings, contracts and cases only. It writes nothing back into your property management system unless you ask for that and the rules allow it. And it decides nothing; a person reads the file and acts.

How it would start with you

With one real object or one real dispute, from your own archive, under a data-processing agreement. You judge the result on a case whose answer you already know, which is the only honest way to test a colleague like this.

The first phase leaves you with your files converted into a checked, machine-readable archive, yours whatever you decide afterwards. Property portfolios are exactly the kind of paper that will have to be converted sooner or later, and doing it on a small contract against real cases is the cheapest way to have it behind you.

Frida, the document colleague, is in preparation and the first client engagement is being prepared now. Whoever writes now has a say in which files she works on first.
